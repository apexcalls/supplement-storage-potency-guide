Supplement Storage: How Heat, Light and Moisture Destroy Potency

Supplements are more fragile than they look. Heat, light, moisture and air all degrade them — slowly, silently, and well before the expiry date if storage is poor. A product kept in the wrong place can lose a significant share of its potency while still looking perfectly fine. Storage is not a minor detail; it is part of using the product correctly.

Heat is the biggest enemy. A bottle left in a hot car, on a sunny windowsill, or above a cooker ages far faster than the label assumes. Most supplements are stability-tested at room temperature, and sustained heat accelerates the chemical reactions that break active ingredients down. Probiotics are especially vulnerable: live cultures die quickly at high temperatures, and a bottle that spent a summer in a warm room may contain far fewer live organisms than its label claims.

Light is the second enemy, particularly for oils and for light-sensitive vitamins. Amber and opaque bottles exist for a reason: they block the wavelengths that degrade contents. A clear bottle stored on a bright shelf is a product ageing in plain sight. Keep bottles in their original packaging, in a dark place, and do not decant them into clear containers for convenience.

Moisture is the third enemy. Bathrooms are the worst common storage location: steam from showers raises humidity daily, and moisture can cause tablets to soften, powders to clump, and capsules to stick together. Desiccant packets inside the bottle help, but only if the bottle is kept closed and dry. A cool, dry cupboard in a bedroom is almost always a better choice than a bathroom cabinet.

Air matters too. Every time a bottle is opened, oxygen enters, and oxidation degrades oils and some vitamins. Keep bottles tightly closed, do not leave them open on a counter, and avoid buying quantities so large that the bottle stays open for many months. Smaller bottles, finished sooner, are often the better choice — even if the larger bottle looks cheaper per serving.

Finally, follow the label's own storage instructions. "Store in a cool, dry place" is not filler; it is the condition the expiry date was calculated under. Refrigeration is required for some probiotics and recommended for some oils — but only where the label says so, since cold can damage other products. And remember that an expiry date assumes correct storage: a product stored badly may be past its useful life long before its printed date.
